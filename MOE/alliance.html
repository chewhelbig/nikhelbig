Principle 2 of 5

Therapeutic Alliance

I and Thou, in the here-and-now.

What it is

Martin Buber described two ways of relating. In I–It, I treat the other as an object — a case, a diagnosis, a problem to manage. In I–Thou, I meet the other as a whole person, present and alive, in this moment. Gestalt locates healing in genuine I–Thou contact. It is not rapport as a technique; it cannot be manufactured. It can only be offered, and allowed to happen.

Why it matters for school counsellors

Young clients are exquisitely sensitive to being treated as a "case". A real, present, mutual alliance is the single strongest predictor of whether counselling helps. For students who have been let down by adults, your willingness to meet them as a Thou — not a file — is itself reparative.

In the room

Putting down the assessment form and simply being with the student. Letting yourself be moved, and letting it show appropriately. Speaking from "I" — "something shifts in me when you say that" — rather than only assessing. And repairing ruptures honestly: "I think I pushed too hard last time."

Try this

In your next session, notice the moment you slip into I–It — managing, diagnosing, planning your next move. Gently return to the person in front of you, and name one genuine present-moment response aloud.
