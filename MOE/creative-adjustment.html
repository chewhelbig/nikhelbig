Principle 4 of 5

Creative Adjustment

A child's survival mechanism.

What it is

Creative adjustment is the organism's ongoing way of finding the best possible contact with a difficult environment. A child in an unsafe or depriving field develops strategies — withdrawal, vigilance, pleasing, defiance, numbing — that were genuinely intelligent solutions at the time. The difficulty is that they become fixed, and outlive the situation that once needed them.

Why it matters for school counsellors

This reframes "problem behaviour" as survival intelligence. The anxious perfectionist, the class clown, the shut-down child — each found a way to cope with their field. Honouring the adjustment, rather than simply trying to remove it, respects the child and opens the door to gentler, more lasting change.

In the room

Curiosity in place of correction: "This must have helped you once — what did it protect you from?" Helping a student notice that an old, fixed adjustment may no longer fit their present field, and that new options are now available to them.

Try this

Take a behaviour you find difficult in a student. Ask: what might this have been a creative solution to? How does it make sense as survival? Let that change how you meet them next.
