Principle 5 of 5

Creative Indifference

Holding differences.

What it is

From Salomo Friedlaender, an early influence on Perls, comes the idea of a still, centred zero-point that sits between opposites and can hold both without collapsing into one. This is not indifference as not-caring; it is a balanced, fertile centre from which both poles of a polarity — strength and vulnerability, anger and love, wanting to stay and wanting to leave — can be held at once. From here, opposites are not problems to resolve but energies to integrate.

Why it matters for school counsellors

Young people, and the adults around them, often get stuck in one pole: "I'm all bad," "I hate her — I need her." Your capacity to hold both sides without taking one over the other gives the student room to feel the whole of their experience. It also keeps you steady amid competing pulls — the school's, the parents', the child's.

In the room

Not rushing to resolve ambivalence. "Part of you wants to go back to class, and part of you really doesn't — and both are true." Staying centred when a student swings between extremes, and holding your own neutrality between a child and a frustrated teacher.

Try this

When you feel pulled to one side of a student's conflict, find your centre and name both poles equally. Notice what becomes possible when neither side has to win.
