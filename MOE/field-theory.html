Principle 3 of 5

Field Theory

The whole is different from the sum of its parts.

What it is

Kurt Lewin held that behaviour is a function of the person and their environment taken together — a single, dynamic field. The field is the total situation, all of it interrelated and in motion. A child's behaviour is not a fixed property of the child; it is an event in a field of relationships — family, classroom, peers, teachers, you, this moment. Change one part and the whole reorganises.

Why it matters for school counsellors

School counsellors work at the crossroads of many systems. Field thinking stops you from locating the entire problem inside the child, and reveals leverage you would otherwise miss — a teacher's tone, a seating change, a sibling's illness, even the time of day. You are part of the field too: your own state shapes what becomes possible.

In the room

Mapping who and what surrounds the child before judging the behaviour. A "disruptive" student may be carrying tension that really belongs to the family or the classroom. Reframing a difficult behaviour as a sensible response to its field, then asking where else change could enter.

Try this

For any case, sketch the child at the centre and map the field around them — family, teachers, peers, you. Ask: what is this behaviour solving within this field? Where else could I intervene?
