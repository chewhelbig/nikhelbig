The transcultural orientation of psychology recognises that human psychological experience cannot be understood apart from its cultural context. How people experience the self, relate to others, express suffering, and seek healing are all shaped by culture.

For psychotherapists working across cultures — as is increasingly common in our globalised world — this awareness is not optional but essential. A therapeutic approach developed in one cultural context may not translate directly to another without careful adaptation and sensitivity.
